3. Why these forms?

Diffusion: a Gaussian stays Gaussian, but its width grows as √(σ² + 2Dt); its height decreases to conserve area.

Advection: the original shape travels intact: f(x − vt).

Wave: for zero initial velocity, one pulse splits into two half-amplitude pulses traveling at ±c.

Local relaxation: every position follows the same ODE uₜ = −ku, so the shape only shrinks in height.

4. When does the model change?

Add a spatial boundary and its conditions, and the appropriate modes may change: sine waves for fixed ends; cosine modes for insulated ends.

Add both transport and diffusion and obtain uₜ + v uₓ = D uₓₓ (constant coefficients). Add sources or nonlinear reaction and another term appears.

For steady fields with no local source, the time derivative vanishes and ∇²u = 0 (Laplace equation). With local source, use a Poisson equation.
