MINI DAW — FROM NOTES TO SOUND

Edit events on a musical grid, listen to them as a synthesizer generates audio, change the signal processors, and inspect the actual waveform. All sound is generated locally by your browser; no samples, logins or external libraries.

01 · Transport / Musical time

One bar · 4/4 · 16 sixteenth-note steps

BAR 1 · BEAT 1
Stopped — press Play to enable audio
At 120 BPM, one beat = 0.5 s; one step = 0.125 s; this four-beat bar = 2 s. Notes and drum hits are scheduled ahead of the audio clock, not driven directly by screen repainting.
02 · MIDI / Musical events

Piano roll · Lead synth

PURPLE = NOTE EVENTS

Click cells to enter or remove notes. One pitch per sixteenth-note step. These cells store pitch and time—not audio samples.

Bass notes

Each note sustains for approximately three steps.
03 · MIDI / Drum sequencer

Rhythm pattern

TEAL = TRIGGERS

Drum triggers are also events. The browser synthesizes a kick, snare and closed hi-hat when each event fires.

04 · Instrument / Effects

What turns MIDI into audio?

MIDI note→Oscillator→Envelope→Low-pass→Mixer

Sound design experiment

Sawtooth → low cutoff: fewer upper harmonics. Increase attack: a slower onset. Increase release: a longer decay. These controls change audio processing, not the underlying MIDI notes.

05 · Mixer / Signal routing

Three audio channels to one master

Lead + Bass + Drums→Master→Speakers
Post-fader copies→Delay / echo→Master

The echo is a parallel send/return. Turning it up leaves the dry signal present while adding delayed copies.

06 · Audio samples / Real-time engine

Actual output waveform

48 kHz example: 128 frames ≈ 2.67 ms · actual context not started

The oscilloscope shows sampled output amplitude. Browser audio may use a different hardware sample rate and block schedule. A missed real-time deadline produces an audible glitch.

07 · Understanding the abstractions

Same project, three different kinds of data

Purple · MIDI
Discrete events: note number, start time, duration and velocity. MIDI notes have no sound without an instrument.
Teal · Audio
Continuously generated or recorded pressure-wave samples, processed in time-ordered buffers.
Orange · Control
Values such as gain and filter cutoff. Automation changes processor parameters over time rather than being sound itself.

This deliberately small DAW models the essential abstractions. Each melodic step is monophonic, with fixed velocity, and each song is a repeating single-bar loop. Real DAWs add tracks, editing, recording, plugin graphs, synchronization, sample libraries and arbitrary arrangements. Browser playback requires a Play click.
